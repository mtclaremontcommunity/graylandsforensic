For journalists

Covering the Graylands Campus Project

A sourced fact sheet and image library for reporting on the Graylands forensic mental health campus expansion in Mount Claremont, Western Australia.

A forensic mental health campus set to nearly double in size — closing every general psychiatric bed on the site in the process — sits within a kilometre of five schools, one of them, John XXIII College, on its direct boundary. Site works began in September 2026. Infrastructure WA, the government's own statutory infrastructure advisor, found the funding case inadequate in March 2023; government committed $218.9 million three weeks later, while its own Budget Papers still described that business case as unfinished. No consultation with residents or nearby schools has been identified across five years of planning — though government ran exactly that process, advertised and dated, the last time forensic capacity grew on this site, in 1993.

Position, quotable in full

Mount Claremont Community Network supports properly resourced forensic mental health services in Western Australia. Our position is Pause, Publish, Consult: pause site works, publish the evidence behind the decision, and hold genuine community consultation — before Stage 1, a $197.4 million first phase the government's own tender describes as part of a long-term plan once costed at $698 million, locks in the path for everything that follows.

What we are not saying

  • Not that the project should be cancelled.
  • Not that forensic mental health patients are dangerous as a class.
  • Not opposed to mental health services at Graylands, which has provided them since 1909.
  • Not that harm will occur — the claim is narrower: nobody has published the evidence to show it won't, and a government that overrides its own independent infrastructure advisor carries the burden of showing its work.

The facts

Five schools, a campus about to nearly double, no consultation on the record. John XXIII College shares its boundary with the site; four more schools sit within a kilometre. The campus grows from around 45 forensic beds to 176, and every remaining general psychiatric bed closes. Asked in its September 2026 fact sheet what consultation had occurred, government named clinical experts and lived-experience groups — not residents, not the schools. No formal local consultation appears anywhere in the public record across five years of planning.

The state's own infrastructure adviser found the case inadequate. It was funded three weeks later. In March 2023, Infrastructure WA assessed the business case and found it had "insufficient information on which to base an investment decision." Government committed $218.9 million about three weeks after that finding. Its own Budget Papers record the money as "provisioned" while the Stage 1 business case was still "under development for future Government consideration." No document explaining what had changed has been published.

Government consulted residents before Frankland opened in 1993. Nothing equivalent has happened this time. Before Graylands' first secure forensic unit admitted a patient, the Health Minister publicly promised the community would be consulted on admission guidelines first, then followed through: newspaper advertisements, letters and pamphlets to residents, and a dated deadline for public comment. Thirty-three years later, a change many times larger — the whole site converting to forensic use — has had no equivalent process identified. Longer account, sources and photographs: the history page.

The site sits inside a sewage-treatment odour buffer the council's own rules say should exclude it. The City of Nedlands' planning scheme excludes "sensitive land uses," hospitals included, from the mapped buffer around the Subiaco Wastewater Treatment Plant. Asked in Parliament in 2025 whether a risk assessment had been done, the responsible Minister's full answer was one sentence, with no modelling or conclusion volunteered. Full record, including the planning clause and the Hansard exchange: the odour buffer page.

Also on the record: WA's own psychiatrists describe the state's forensic mental health system as "gridlocked," and in April 2026 the Supreme Court found it "impossible" to properly apply the state's mental impairment law because there's no secure hospital capacity — a man found not criminally responsible for a killing is being held in prison indefinitely as a result ABC News, 27 Apr 2026. This campaign does not dispute that WA needs more forensic capacity; its position is that concentrating it at this site, at this scale, hasn't been shown to be the right way to provide it.

Background: how the site has changed

1909–1993
A general hospital, with no purpose-built secure unit.
1993–2026
A general hospital with a small secure wing — the Frankland Centre, 30 beds — attached, opened seven years after John XXIII College arrived on the boundary.
2026 onwards
General psychiatric care closes entirely. The site converts to a fully forensic campus, under a community-leave law that didn't exist when the site was chosen in 2021.
