THE PAPER TRAIL

Every contract on the record

This page grew out of the published timeline on the site page. That page tells the story in a few dated lines; this one is the full paper trail behind it — every contract, every firm, every dollar, sourced to Tenders WA with a saved copy of each record.

Follow the money

Every Graylands contract on the public record

Twenty-eight contracts for the Graylands reconfiguration and forensic expansion appear on Tenders WA, the WA Government's public contracts register, awarded between October 2022 and August 2026. This is the full scope of each one, the firm that got it, how it was awarded, how long it runs and what it cost. Every reference number below links to a saved copy of the original register record, so nothing here depends on our transcription. Twenty firms have been paid to plan, design and begin enabling this expansion. Main hospital construction is not among them — the one exception is a small, separate fit-out of an existing heritage building, set out below.

28 contracts published on Tenders WA, Oct 2022 to Aug 2026

$504,418 added to 11 contracts by variation after they were awarded

How to read this table

  • The $8,958,992 is not the cost of the project. It is the total value of contracts awarded for planning, advice, early contractor involvement and one small heritage fit-out. Main hospital construction has not been contracted. The announced project figure is separate and far larger.
  • 11 contracts have been revised upward after award, adding $504,418. The largest movements are the NMHS consultancy on service delivery models, up 230% from $62,150 to $205,150, the environmental services contract, up 241% from $35,937 to $122,709, and the environmental engineering work at the Claremont Therapeutic Riding Centre, up 131% from $30,000 to $69,325. 3 contracts were revised down. The register records the movement but not the reason for it.
  • Seven contracts publish an estimate rather than a fixed price, including the $5,619,001 early contractor involvement contract. Estimates may settle higher or lower.
  • Nine contracts publish actual expenditure, shown in the value column where available. This is what was really spent, and it is not always the contract value.
  • "How awarded" matters, but read it in context. A Common Use Arrangement or panel means the buyer selects from firms already on a standing government list — the standard method WA agencies use for recurring specialist work like architecture, engineering and cost management, not something distinctive to this project. Direct and limited sourcing mean the work was offered to one or a few chosen firms rather than the open market. Most contracts here were awarded one of these three ways; one — NMHS202414235, the health service facility planning consultancy — was openly advertised and drew 13 submissions. The register records how many submissions each contract received, not how many firms were invited to bid, so a single submission on a panel or limited-sourcing contract isn't evidence of a closed process on its own.
  • The Fortescue House work is separate from the new build. Three contracts cover refurbishing the heritage-listed Fortescue House and relocating the patient cashier service into it, totalling more than $250,000.

What this record actually shows.

This isn't a claim that nobody has looked at anything. Twenty firms have been paid to develop a business case, plan the site, and examine security, environmental and heritage questions during the PDP. The question this campaign is asking isn't whether that work happened — the register shows that it did. It's what those studies concluded, whether any of them retested the suitability of this site once the law changed in 2023, and why the findings haven't been shown to the community living beside it.
