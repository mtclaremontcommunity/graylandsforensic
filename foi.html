A parallel track

Asking government to show its work

Everything on this site so far is what government chose to release. Freedom of Information is the one tool that compels an agency to search its own records and answer, in writing, on a legal deadline — whether the answer is convenient or not.

Every letter, every reply, every document you've read on this site so far came into the public domain because government decided to put it there. That's fine for accuracy, but it means government has controlled the record up to this point.

The Freedom of Information Act 1992 (WA) works differently. It's a legal right, not a request for a favour: the agency must search its records, decide within 45 days, give written reasons for any refusal, and have that refusal reviewed independently if asked. It doesn't need a Minister to feel like replying, or a journalist to take an interest.

We've turned that right into a specific, developed plan — eight applications, aimed at the questions this campaign still can't answer from what's already public. Doing it properly takes real time and, in places, real money. That's what this page sets out.

The eight applications

We're not publishing the application text itself — an FOI request reads best as plain administrative paperwork, not a campaign document. What follows is what each one asks for, and why.

REQUEST 01 OF 08 Planning pathway Approvals and remaining public comment opportunities

To: Department of Planning, Lands and Heritage / WA Planning Commission; Department of Finance (Building Management and Works).

Our planning has assumed a development application will eventually open a statutory public submission period. That assumption has never been tested, and it may be wrong. This is the most time-critical question in the whole programme, because what everyone else should be doing next depends on the answer.

REQUEST 02 OF 08 Site selection Options assessment and due diligence

To: Department of Health; Mental Health Commission.

Infrastructure WA's own report says twenty sites were assessed on accessibility, central location and public transport, with nothing published on safety, land use compatibility or school proximity. This asks for the working underneath that finding, and whether anyone responsible for planning, policing, justice or fire safety was ever asked whether the site was suitable.

REQUEST 03 OF 08 Model of care Patient composition, leave and reintegration framework

To: North Metropolitan Health Service; Department of Health.

We are not asking about any individual patient. We're asking what security classifications will apply, how community leave is authorised, what geographic boundaries govern it, and who oversees those decisions — the same operational variables government already uses for its own service planning.

REQUEST 04 OF 08 Capacity & demand Current occupancy, demand and workforce

To: North Metropolitan Health Service.

Tests whether the case for a 136-bed expansion is being made from current, published figures — or from projections nobody outside government has seen.

REQUEST 05 OF 08 Existing campus Condition, compliance and capacity

To: North Metropolitan Health Service.

A $698 million expansion should be able to point to a documented case for it. This asks whether a condition and compliance assessment of what's already on site actually exists.

REQUEST 06 OF 08 Engagement records Community and stakeholder engagement

To: Department of Health; both responsible Ministers.

What was actually said to the City of Nedlands and nearby schools, when, and by whom — set against what the government's own current mental health strategy says infrastructure consultation is supposed to look like.

REQUEST 07 OF 08 Independent oversight Chief Psychiatrist oversight and advice

To: Department of Health / Office of the Chief Psychiatrist.

WA's Chief Psychiatrist is an independent statutory office with oversight of psychiatric services. This asks what advice, if any, that office has given on this project.

REQUEST 08 OF 08 The IWA finding Infrastructure WA's assessment, and what followed it

To: Department of Health; Infrastructure WA.

Infrastructure WA found the business case contained "insufficient information on which to base an investment decision." This asks what happened between that finding and the funding decision three weeks later.

What it costs

Under the Freedom of Information Act 1992 (WA), a non-personal application costs a fixed $30 to lodge. Beyond that, an agency can charge for the time spent locating and processing documents, but must give a written estimate first if that's likely to exceed $25 — and financially disadvantaged applicants can have processing charges reduced Office of the Information Commissioner WA.

Based on the scope of these eight applications, our own estimate — working from that fee schedule — is $420 in fixed fees plus somewhere between $500 and $1,500 in processing charges, depending on how much material each agency actually holds. We keep that down by asking for electronic copies only, conceding redactions that don't matter, and responding to a high estimate by narrowing the request rather than paying it or walking away. Realistically, we're budgeting for a total of $900 to $1,400 across the full programme.

If we can't fund all eight, we'd rather run three or four properly than run eight badly. The planning-pathway and site-selection questions carry the most public value for the least cost, and would go first regardless of what we raise.

What happens with what we get back

Anything released will be published on this site in full, alongside the agency's own decision letter, in the same side-by-side style already used for the correspondence we've received on the Correspondence page — the primary sources speak for themselves, and we won't characterise them for you. A refusal isn't a dead end either: an agency has to name the specific legal ground it's relying on, and that decision can be reviewed — first inside the agency, then independently by the Information Commissioner, free of charge.

None of this replaces the Legislative Council petition — it runs alongside it. If you haven't yet, that's still the fastest way to register support.
