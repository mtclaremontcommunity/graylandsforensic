A parallel track

Asking government to show its work

Everything on this site so far is what government chose to release. Freedom of Information is the one tool that compels an agency to search its own records and answer, in writing, on a legal deadline — whether the answer is convenient or not.

Every letter, every reply, every document you've read on this site so far came into the public domain because government decided to put it there. That's fine for accuracy, but it means government has controlled the record up to this point.

The Freedom of Information Act 1992 (WA) works differently. It's a legal right, not a request for a favour: the agency must search its records, decide within 45 days, give written reasons for any refusal, and have that refusal reviewed independently if asked. It doesn't need a Minister to feel like replying, or a journalist to take an interest.

We've turned that right into a specific, developed plan — nine applications, aimed at the questions this campaign still can't answer from what's already public. Doing it properly takes real time and, in places, real money. That's what this page sets out.

The applications

We're not publishing the application text itself — an FOI request reads best as plain administrative paperwork, not a campaign document. What follows is what each one asks for, and why. Two subjects are split into paired applications so the more urgent or lower-risk half isn't held up by the harder one.

REQUEST 01A Planning pathway Approvals and remaining public comment opportunities

To: Department of Planning, Lands and Heritage / WA Planning Commission; DTMI (Office of Major Infrastructure Delivery), which now leads the State's project team for Graylands.

Our planning has assumed a development application will eventually open a statutory public submission period. That assumption has never been tested, and it may be wrong. This is the most time-critical question in the whole programme, because what everyone else should be doing next depends on the answer.

REQUEST 01B Early works The Early Works Agreement and design brief

To: DTMI (Office of Major Infrastructure Delivery).

Split out from Request 01 because it involves contractor and design content that shouldn't hold up the more urgent planning-pathway answer above. Covers the enabling-works approval behind the Claremont Therapeutic Riding Centre relocation and the HV, communications, water and fire main upgrades now visible at the John XXIII Avenue boundary.

REQUEST 02 Site selection Filed — 26 Aug 2026 Options assessment and due diligence

To: Department of Health; Mental Health Commission.

Infrastructure WA's own report says twenty sites were assessed on accessibility, central location and public transport, with nothing published on safety, land use compatibility or school proximity. This asks for the working underneath that finding, and whether anyone responsible for planning, policing, justice or fire safety was ever asked whether the site was suitable.

Status: Lodged with the Department of Health (FOI 2026-55). This particular application is narrowly scoped, so a nil result or a refusal on part of it wouldn't be a surprise — either way, we'll publish the agency's decision in full once it lands. See the Funding & costs page for how this was paid for.

REQUEST 08A The IWA finding What happened to Infrastructure WA's findings

To: Department of Health; Infrastructure WA.

Infrastructure WA found the business case contained "insufficient information on which to base an investment decision." This asks for the correspondence, the further work done in response, and any close-out or sign-off — the direct evidentiary trail behind funding following three weeks later. Administrative and technical material, not Cabinet-facing, so it's been brought forward rather than held to the back of the programme.

REQUEST 08B The 2025 plan The Project Definition Plan technical and assurance record

To: DTMI (OMID); Department of Health.

A government tender confirms Cabinet approved a Project Definition Plan (PDP) in November 2025, describing its purpose as refining "strategic justification and objectives" and "value for money" — not site suitability. This asks for the PDP itself and any assurance record showing whether it addressed the Infrastructure WA findings, the 2023 law change, or the surrounding community — expressly excluding any Cabinet submission about it. Higher exemption risk than 08A; if Cabinet privilege is claimed, we've asked the agency to say which specific parts meet the test rather than accept it at face value.

REQUEST 03 Model of care Patient composition, leave and reintegration framework

To: North Metropolitan Health Service; Department of Health.

We are not asking about any individual patient, and we're not asking for exact leave routes or itineraries — only the policy or framework governing where community leave can occur, if one exists. We're asking what security classifications will apply, how community leave is authorised, and who oversees those decisions.

Status: Filing now, except the leave-authorisation item, which is held pending Sandra Brewer MLA's campus-security Question on Notice — see the Parliamentary questions tracker.

REQUEST 07 Independent oversight Chief Psychiatrist oversight and advice

To: Department of Health / Office of the Chief Psychiatrist.

WA's Chief Psychiatrist is an independent statutory office with authority this campaign can't manufacture on its own. Narrowed to the question that matters most: any formal advice that office has given specifically on this project, and how it was addressed.

REQUEST 09 The odour buffer Whether the buffer fix is funded, dated, and on time

To: Water Corporation, DWER, DTMI (OMID) and City of Nedlands — four separate, tailored applications rather than one form letter, since each agency holds a different piece of this.

We're not asking whether the odour buffer is a problem — Infrastructure WA already said it is, and asked in Parliament, the responsible Minister confirmed it "has been considered as part of the project definition plan," without saying what that found. We're asking for the one thing that resolves it either way: whether a funded, dated fix exists, or whether approval is proceeding inside the buffer as currently mapped.

See The odour buffer question for the full background, including the Hansard record.

REQUEST 06 Engagement records Community and stakeholder engagement

To: Department of Health; DTMI (OMID).

Narrowed to the finished plan, register and log — not five years of raw correspondence, which mainly generates a large cost estimate rather than an answer. Asks whether a community engagement plan exists at all, and tests the government's own claim that consultation meant clinical experts, a Lived Experience Advisory Group and a Clinical Advisory Group, by asking what supplemented that for residents, schools and local government.

REQUEST 04 Background Current capacity, demand and workforce

To: North Metropolitan Health Service.

Lower priority than it once was. High demand and stretched capacity would mostly support the case for more forensic beds somewhere — which this campaign has never disputed. Useful context, not core leverage, so it's filed last if resources allow.

REQUEST 05 Background Existing campus condition and compliance

To: North Metropolitan Health Service.

Held back deliberately. If the existing Frankland Centre is shown to be genuinely deteriorating, that's evidence for the government's case that new facilities are needed — it doesn't answer why Graylands, or why this scale and model at this site. Kept as optional background rather than core.

What it costs

Under the Freedom of Information Act 1992 (WA), a non-personal application costs a fixed $30 to lodge. Beyond that, an agency can charge for the time spent locating and processing documents, but must give a written estimate first if that's likely to exceed $25 — and financially disadvantaged applicants can have processing charges reduced Office of the Information Commissioner WA.

Based on the scope of these applications, our own estimate — working from that fee schedule — is $600 in fixed fees (20 lodgements at $30 each, since several requests go to more than one agency, and two subjects are split in two) plus somewhere between $400 and $1,000 in processing charges, depending on how much material each agency actually holds. We keep that down by asking for electronic copies only, conceding redactions that don't matter, and responding to a high estimate by narrowing the request rather than paying it or walking away. Realistically, we're budgeting for a total of $1,000 to $1,600 across the full programme.

If we can't fund everything, we'd rather run the planning-pathway, site-selection and Infrastructure WA questions properly than run all eleven applications badly. Those carry the most public value for the least cost, and go first regardless of what we raise. The two background requests (04, 05) are the first to drop if funds are tight.

What happens with what we get back

Anything released will be published on this site in full, alongside the agency's own decision letter, in the same side-by-side style already used for the correspondence we've received on the Correspondence page — the primary sources speak for themselves, and we won't characterise them for you. A refusal isn't a dead end either: an agency has to name the specific legal ground it's relying on, and that decision can be reviewed — first inside the agency, then independently by the Information Commissioner, free of charge.

None of this replaces the Legislative Council petition — it runs alongside it. If you haven't yet, that's still the fastest way to add your name to this.
